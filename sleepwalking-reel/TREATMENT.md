# How Sleepwalking Works — Treatment

Style: **Low-poly Isometric Island** (`lowpoly-island`). ~60 s, 1080×1920 (9:16), 24 fps, English narration + captions, −14 LUFS.

## Facts (checked)
- Sleepwalking is a partial arousal from NREM sleep, typically in the **first third of the night** (RACGP, Aust Fam Physician 2017).
- Intracerebral EEG during an episode from **slow-wave sleep (N3)**: the **motor cortex and cingulate** showed wake-like fast activity while the **frontal and parietal association cortices** kept deep-sleep delta waves (Terzaghi et al. 2009, PMC2647795).
- 12-month prevalence: **5.0 % of children, 1.5 % of adults**; lifetime 6.9 % (Stallman & Kohler 2016, PLoS ONE meta-analysis, 51 studies).
- Recall ranges from complete amnesia to full recall; usually little or none.
- Runs in families (twin studies). Triggers named in reviews: sleep loss, stress, fever, some medications, sleep-disordered breathing.
- Waking a sleepwalker is not harmful but can confuse them; they "can be led back to bed and do not need to be awakened" (RACGP).

## Three structures
1. **One night, one house** — a sleeper's night as a time-lapse on one floating island house; brain cutaways are inserted as zooms.
2. **Brain as the world** — the whole film inside a low-poly brain whose regions are islands.
3. **Night clock** — a hex-column hypnogram grows through the night and opens into each scene.

**Chosen: 1, with 3 inside it.** The house at night is the stage (emissive windows, moon), the camera zooms *through the sky* into the brain island and the night-graph island, then back to the house; dawn floods the world at the end (light as clock).

## Sound
Event-driven score: marimba/vibraphone/celesta notes on every arrival, soft pad; pentatonic. Night bed: crickets, low wind; a clock tick. Silence (pad only) before the brain lights. Dawn: birds and the score resolves.
